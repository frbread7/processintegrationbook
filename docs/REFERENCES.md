# References and source use

The following sources support specific, bounded statements in the chapters. They are not blanket evidence for every example. Platform descriptions are dated and source-limited. No recipe, unpublished specification, or non-public data is used.

## Public papers and technical background

1. R. B. Fair, “Boron Diffusion in Silicon-Concentration and Orientation Dependence, Background Effects, and Profile Estimation,” *Journal of The Electrochemical Society*, 122(6), 800–805 (1975). [DOI: 10.1149/1.2134326](https://doi.org/10.1149/1.2134326). The abstract reports concentration-dependent diffusion, orientation effects under oxidizing conditions, and retarded or accelerated diffusion with n-type impurities. Its specific high-concentration statement concerns B profiles above 2 × 10¹⁹ cm⁻³ in neutral ambients over a 550 °C range. The lab warning is a heuristic prompt, not a universal cutoff.
2. H. Bracht, H. H. Silvestri, I. D. Sharp, and E. E. Haller, “Self- and foreign-atom diffusion in semiconductor isotope heterostructures. II. Experimental results for silicon,” *Physical Review B*, 75(3), 035211 (2007). [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevB.75.035211](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.75.035211). The abstract reports B, As, and P diffusion measurements in silicon isotope multilayers at 850–1100 °C and native-point-defect modeling. It states that B diffusion is mainly self-interstitial-mediated under the study's conditions; this is not a calibration of this book's simplified model.
3. B. Sadigh, T. J. Lenosky, S. K. Theiss, M.-J. Caturla, T. Diaz de la Rubia, and M. A. Foad, “Mechanism of Boron Diffusion in Silicon: An Ab Initio and Kinetic Monte Carlo Study,” *Physical Review Letters*, 83(21), 4341–4344 (1999). [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.4341](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.4341). The abstract describes an interstitialcy mechanism from first-principles energetics, molecular dynamics, and kinetic Monte Carlo. It is mechanism research, not process calibration.
4. Y.-C. Yeo, “Metal gate technology for nanoscale transistors—material selection and process integration issues,” *Thin Solid Films*, 462–463, 34–41 (2004). [DOI: 10.1016/j.tsf.2004.05.039](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tsf.2004.05.039). The review discusses metal-gate material requirements, metal–dielectric interface physics, and integration issues. It does not establish a target-platform thermal or chemical process margin.
5. R. Aitken, V. Chandra, B. Cline, S. Das, D. Pietromonaco, L. Shifren, S. Sinha, and G. Yeric, “Predicting future complementary metal–oxide–semiconductor technology – challenges and approaches,” *IET Computers & Digital Techniques*, 10(6), 315–322 (2016). [DOI: 10.1049/iet-cdt.2015.0210](https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-cdt.2015.0210). The paper discusses predictive technology and design-technology co-optimization (DTCO), including feedback between technology assumptions and design outcomes; it does not validate any hypothetical PDK or process option in this book.
6. K. Mistry et al., “A 45nm Logic Technology with High-k+Metal Gate Transistors, Strained Silicon, 9 Cu Interconnect Layers, 193nm Dry Patterning, and 100% Pb-free Packaging,” in *2007 IEEE International Electron Devices Meeting*, 247–250 (2007). [DOI: 10.1109/IEDM.2007.4418914](https://doi.org/10.1109/IEDM.2007.4418914). Chapter 13's high-k-first/metal-gate-last sequence and the paper's stated workfunction/thermal-budget rationale are checked against Figure 5 and surrounding text on pp. 247–248. The sequence is a historical public platform report, not a universal flow. Engineering burdens and proposed transfer tests are explicitly labeled as inferences.
7. R. H. Esser, K. D. Hobart, and F. J. Kub, “Direct Wafer Bonded Abrupt Junction Tunnel Diodes,” *Journal of The Electrochemical Society*, 151(6), G387–G390 (2004). [DOI: 10.1149/1.1731558](https://doi.org/10.1149/1.1731558). Page G387 prints the boron-in-silicon values D₀ = 0.76 and Eₐ = 3.46 eV in its diffusion calculation. Page G390 reference 10 cites S. M. Sze, *VLSI Technology* (1983), as a historical source; that book's original page/table was not independently verified. The paper does not state D₀'s unit in that sentence; the cm²/s unit follows from the diffusivity equation and is explicit in the corroborating review below.
8. A. Mostafa and M. Medraj, “Binary Phase Diagrams and Thermodynamic Properties of Silicon and Essential Doping Elements (Al, As, B, Bi, Ga, In, N, P, Sb and Tl),” *Materials*, 10(6), 676 (2017). [DOI: 10.3390/ma10060676](https://doi.org/10.3390/ma10060676); [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5554057/). The boron–silicon section reproduces the intrinsic diffusivity equation with D₀ = 0.76 cm²/s and Eₐ = 3.46 eV. The review cites W. J. Scotten, *Diffusion in Silicon* (IC Knowledge, 2000) as ref. 26; that historical source's original chapter/page is not independently verified here. The review corroborates the reported parameterization but not its original provenance or process calibration.
9. S. M. Sze (ed.), *VLSI Technology*, McGraw-Hill, New York (1983), ISBN 0-07-062686-3. [Library catalog record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL3504531M/VLSI_technology). This is the historical book cited as reference 10 by Esser et al.; a page/table locator in the book was not independently verified and is not asserted here.
10. S. M. Sze and K. K. Ng, *Physics of Semiconductor Devices*, 3rd ed., Wiley (2007). [Publisher DOI: 10.1002/0470068329](https://doi.org/10.1002/0470068329). General semiconductor-device background only; this edition is not cited as the source for the selected Arrhenius pair.

## Reconstruction method sources

11. V. Ivrii, [*Partial Differential Equations*, Chapter 3, half-line heat-equation visualization](https://www.math.toronto.edu/ivrii/PDE-textbook/Chapter3/S3.1.V.html), University of Toronto. The accessible page explicitly gives the plus-image Green function for the zero-gradient Neumann problem. It supports the mathematical reflecting-boundary construction in c06, not the physical assertion that a real implant surface is impermeable. Page inspected 2026-10-19.
12. NIST, [CODATA 2018 recommended constants, full table](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/all_2018.pdf). The table gives the exact energy-equivalent value of Boltzmann's constant as 8.617333262… × 10⁻⁵ eV/K, used in c06. Table inspected 2026-10-19; the model uses a finite rounded value.
13. Q. Li, S.-M. Koo, H. D. Xiong, M. D. Edelstein, J. S. Suehle, X. Zhu, D. E. Ioannou, and C. A. Richter, [“Methods to Characterize the Electrical and Mechanical Properties of Si Nanowires”](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=32634), NIST-hosted paper, pp. 1–2. Its Eq. (1), Figure 2, and Kelvin discussion show how an intercept and a length-dependent slope can separate contact and path contributions for the paper's *nanowire* geometry. This is method context for c08; it does not validate the chapter's idealized planar-sheet fit or synthetic values. Relevant full-text pages inspected 2026-10-19.
14. G. K. Reeves and H. B. Harrison, “Obtaining the specific contact resistance from transmission line model measurements,” *IEEE Electron Device Letters* 3(5), 111–113 (1982), [DOI 10.1109/EDL.1982.25502](https://doi.org/10.1109/EDL.1982.25502). Publisher-indexed metadata and abstract only were inspected; the abstract notes the importance of contact-end resistance when sheet resistance under a contact differs. No unseen equation or numerical result is attributed to this paper.
15. H. Aharoni, “Stacking faults in silicon epitaxial layers,” *Vacuum* 26(4–5), 167–180 (1976), [DOI 10.1016/S0042-207X(76)80006-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0042-207X(76)80006-1). Publisher-indexed abstract inspected; full text was not. Its (111) silicon-epi examples distinguish possible substrate, interface, and growth-associated origins, but no particular pit or particle appearance in this book is assigned to one.
16. K. Minowa, K. Takeda, S. Tomimatsu, and K. Umemura, “TEM observation of grown-in defects in CZ and epitaxial silicon wafers detected with optical shallow defect analyzer,” *Journal of Crystal Growth* 210(1–3), 15–19 (2000), [DOI 10.1016/S0022-0248(99)00638-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0248(99)00638-7). Publisher-indexed abstract inspected; full text was not. Optical-defect dimensions varied against TEM in the reported experiment, supporting site-specific structural follow-up rather than morphology-based cause assignment.
17. [IUCr Online Dictionary of Crystallography, “Stacking fault”](https://dictionary.iucr.org/Stacking_fault), exact entry inspected 2026-10-19. It classifies a stacking fault as a planar defect; c04 uses that definition without reproducing its text or diagram.
18. A. T. Fiory et al., “Transient-Enhanced Diffusion in Shallow-Junction Formation,” *Journal of Electronic Materials* 31(10), 999–1003 (2002), [DOI 10.1007/s11664-002-0034-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11664-002-0034-6); [author-hosted paper](https://web.njit.edu/~sirenko/PapersNJIT/Ravi_JEM_2002.pdf). Full-text pp. 999–1001 inspected 2026-10-19: the study distinguishes SIMS chemical profiles, sheet-resistance/Hall activation, and diode leakage in low-energy implanted silicon under its own conditions. It does not validate the synthetic split or the lab's simple diffusion coefficient.
19. S. Tian et al., “A detailed physical model for ion implant induced damage in silicon,” *IEEE Transactions on Electron Devices* 45(6), 1226–1238 (1998), [DOI 10.1109/16.678523](https://doi.org/10.1109/16.678523). IEEE publisher-indexed abstract only inspected; full text was not. Its described point-defect/damage model supplies TED context, not an implemented model or calibration here.
