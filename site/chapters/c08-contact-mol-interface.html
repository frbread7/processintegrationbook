Chapter 08

The contact and middle-of-line interface

A contact is a shared interface among activated junctions, surface preparation, contact materials, and local interconnect.

The contact and middle-of-line interface

Decision question: which contact/MOL interface concept meets the electrical and reliability intent without violating the upstream junction, thermal, pattern, or downstream routing constraints?

  • Draw the contact path as a series of interfaces rather than one resistance number.
  • Identify how junction state, preclean, contact formation, and local routing interact.
  • Choose measurements that separate interface resistance from access, geometry, and measurement artifacts.

Trace the complete current path

For a source/drain contact, current crosses the semiconductor surface and its near-surface doping profile, any silicide or other contact-forming layer, a conductive fill and its liner/barrier where used, then local interconnect. The measured resistance also includes spreading/access resistance and geometry. These terms are coupled: a low-resistivity contact material cannot compensate without limit for a depleted or damaged surface, a narrow effective contact, a discontinuous interface, or an access region whose sheet resistance is high.

At a simplified level, a contact contribution may be represented as Rc ≈ ρc/Aeff, where ρc is specific contact resistivity and Aeff is electrically active contact area. This lumped relation assumes reasonably uniform current transfer and is not generally a geometry-independent extraction law. Current crowding introduces a transfer length and makes the perimeter, semiconductor sheet resistance, contact dimensions, and interface distribution relevant. Report the structure and extraction method with any ρc; do not compare values from unlike structures as intrinsic material constants.

Surface preparation illustrates the cross-module trade. A clean may remove native oxide or residues, but its chemistry, selectivity, queue time, and exposure can change surface termination, consume material, or affect junction/interface condition. Contact formation then depends on that starting state and subsequent thermal history. The integration question is the interface contract—allowed surface condition, time-to-deposition, material compatibility, and evidence—not a standalone cleaning recipe. For general contact and metal module foundations, see ProcessBook metal chapter; for BCD-specific context, use PMICBook integration and metrology. This chapter does not reproduce a device-family flow or recipe.

Resolve coupled constraints with evidence

A contact option must coexist with the junction depth and activation established upstream, contact etch alignment and landing margin, thermal exposure after contact formation, and local interconnect resistance/current density. Increasing junction dose may lower access resistance but can change leakage, capacitance, short-channel behavior, or subsequent diffusion. A more aggressive contact etch may clear residue but consume the landing layer or damage the junction. A thicker liner may improve continuity or diffusion blocking while consuming a larger fraction of a scaled opening. These are conditional trade-offs; the acceptable direction depends on structure, materials, geometry, and temperature history.

Use a resistance decomposition as a hypothesis map: Rmeas = Raccess + Rinterface + Rfill/line + Rrouting. It is accounting, not proof that each term can be separately observed. A Kelvin structure reduces some lead contribution but still depends on its layout and current distribution. A transmission-line method can estimate contact and sheet terms only under its geometry and model assumptions. Pair electrical structures with cross-sectional/material analysis when continuity, reaction, or interface chemistry is in question. WATBook treats measurement contract, extraction, and uncertainty; link to WATBook rather than repeating that methodology.

For a uniform-width TLM test pattern, the ideal linear relation is Rtotal = 2Rc + Rsh(L/W): the intercept at L/W = 0 is 2Rc, and the slope is sheet resistance Rsh. In a synthetic fit with intercept 20 Ω and slope 0.80 Ω per unit L/W, the model estimates Rc = 10 Ω per contact and Rsh = 0.80 Ω/□. The units follow because L/W is dimensionless. This interpretation requires comparable contacts, uniform sheet conduction, a valid spacing range, and negligible or modeled parasitics; contact crowding and finite transfer length can require a richer fit. A Kelvin measurement can reduce voltage-lead resistance for its specific structure, but it does not by itself isolate interface chemistry or predict transistor drive current. Report geometry, fit range, residuals, and uncertainty with either extraction.

Worked cross-module decision

Illustrative assumption: a fictional process split shows higher source/drain chain resistance after a revised preclean, while junction leakage is unchanged within the measurement resolution. Two explanations remain: altered interface resistance or a contact-geometry/etch-clearance shift. Do not immediately increase activation anneal or change the contact metal. First compare matched Kelvin/TLM structures with identical geometry, verify the etched landing and liner/fill continuity on representative cross-sections, and review preclean-to-deposition queue and surface evidence. If interface-sensitive structures move but geometry monitors do not, the interface hypothesis gains support; if both move with density or contact dimensions, geometry/access remains plausible. Preserve uncertainty and plan a controlled split before adopting a change.

Knowledge check

A Kelvin contact value improved after a material change. Can the team conclude the full transistor drive current will improve?

No. The measurement supports a change in that structure under its extraction assumptions. Device current also depends on access regions, geometry, mobility, bias, self-heating, and other series elements. Confirm the contact result's comparability, then evaluate transistor-level behavior and relevant reliability constraints.

Further reading: Mistry et al., IEDM 2007 reports a dated 45 nm logic platform with Cu interconnect and its process context; it does not establish a universal contact stack. For measurement limitations, see WATBook chapters on extraction and uncertainty.
